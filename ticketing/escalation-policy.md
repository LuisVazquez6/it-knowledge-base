# Ticket Escalation Policy

## Overview
This document defines the IT support structure, escalation criteria, and requirements

---

## Support Tier Definitions

| Tier | Name | Handles | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | Help Desk | Basic, common issues | Passwords resets, printer issues software issues |\
| Tier 2 | Technical Support | Intermediate issues | Network config | server access |
| Tier 3 | Systems / Network Engineers | Complex Infrastructure | Server failures |
| Tier 4 | Vendors / Specialists | Product-specific issues | Microsoft, Cisco |

---

## When to Escalate

### Required Information When Escalating 


> Always provide ALL of the following before escalating. Incomplete tickets delays resolution

- Full name and department of the affected user
- Device name / asset tag number
- Exact error message (screenshot preferred)
- Date and time the issue started
- Steps already attempted by Tier 1
- Business impact - is the user fully down or partially working?
- Ticket number from the ticket system

---

## Respond Time SLAs (Service Level Agreements)

| Priority | Description | Tier 1 Response | Resolution Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 - Critical | User/system completely down | 15 minutes | 2 hours |
| P2 - High | Major function unavailable | 30 minutes | 4 hours |
| P3 - Medium | Issue with workaround available | 2 hours | Next business day |
| P4 - Low | Minor issue or general request | 4 hours | Within 3 business days |

---

## Escalation Process Step by Step

1. Attempt to resolve the issue at your current tier within the SLA timeframe
2. **Document all troubleshooting steps** taken in the ticket.
3. Notify the user that the ticket is being escalated and provide an estimated response
4. Update the **Ticket Priority** if needed before escalating.
5. Assign the ticket to the appropriate Tier 2 and Tier 3 queue in the ticketing system
6. Send a direct message or email to the Tier 2 technician with a brief summary.
7. **Do not close the ticket** - leave it open and monitor for updates.

---

## Ticket Documentation Standards

Every Ticket must contain clear documentation before escalation or closure:

- Initial issue description as reported by the user
- All troubleshooting steps performed and their results
- Resolution details or reason for escalation 
- Time spend on the ticket
- **User confirmation** that the issue is resolved before closing
