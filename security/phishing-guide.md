# Phishing Identification Guide

## Overview
Phishing is one of the most common Cybersecurity threats facing organizations. This guide helps employees identify phishing emails and respond appropriately to protect company data

## Applies To
- All employees receiving company email
- All devices - company-issued and personal

---

## How to identity a Phishing Email

### Red Flag 1 Suspicious Sender Address
Always check the **full email address**, and not just the display name. Phishing emails often spoof display names but use fake domains.

- ✅ Legitimate: `support@microsoft.com`
- ❌ Phishing: `support@micros0ft-help.com`

### Red Flag 2 - Urgent or threatening Language
Phishing emails create a false sense of urgency to pressure you into acting quickly

Watch for phrases like:
- *"Your account will be suspended in 24 hours"*
- *"Immediate action Required"*
- *"You have been selected for a reward - claim now"*

### Red flag 3 - Unexpected Attachments or Links
Never open Unexpected attachments or click links in emails you were not expecting. **Hover over the links** to preview the actual URL before clicking.

### Red Flag 4 - Generic Greetings
Legitimate emails use your name. Phishing emails often use:
-*"Dear Customer"*
- *"Dear User"*
- *"Hello Account Holder"*

### Red Flag 5 - Poor Grammar and Spelling
Many emails contain spelling mistakes, awkward phrasing, or inconsistent formatting that a legitimate company would not sent.

### Red Flag 6 - Request for sensitive Information
LegitimateIT department and financial institutions will **NEVER** ask for:
- Your password
- Social Security number
- Credit card details
- MFA codes

---

## What To Do If You Receive a Phishing Email

1. **Do NOT** click any links or open any attachments.
2. **Do NOT** reply to the email or contact the sender.
3. **Report** the email to IT using the reporting button in your email client, or forward it to `security@yourcompany.com`.
4. **Delete** the email from your inbox and empty the trash.
5. If you **accidentally clicked a link**, disconnect from the internet immediately and call IT.

> ⚠️ **If you clicked a phishing link or entered your credentials — call IT immediately. Do not wait.**

## Phishing vs Legitimate Email — Quick Reference

| Characteristic | Legitimate Email | Phishing Email |
|---|---|---|
| Sender address | Matches company domain exactly | Misspelled or random domain |
| Tone | Professional and calm | Urgent, threatening, or alarming |
| Links | Match visible text and company domain | Mismatched or shortened URLs |
| Attachments | Expected and relevant | Unexpected .zip, .exe, or Office files |
| Requests | Never asks for passwords | Asks for credentials or personal info |
| Greeting | Uses your full name | Generic (Dear User, Dear Customer) |

---

## Escalation

All suspected phishing emails must be reported to IT **regardless of whether a link was clicked**.

If a user clicked a link or entered credentials, treat this as a **security incident** and escalate to **Tier 2 immediately**.

Include when escalating:
- Copy or screenshot of the phishing email
- Date and time it was received
- Whether any links were clicked or credentials were entered
- Affected username and device name
